A Little About Me

Curiosity, Creativity, and Connection

I've spent my high school years at Mentor College in Mississauga, Ontario, where I've learned that growth happens at the intersection of discipline and curiosity. Music taught me patience — the kind that comes from showing up to practice even when a piece feels impossible.

Off the stage, you'll find me on the badminton court as team captain, in the kitchen experimenting with recipes, or at a desk folding paper into creatures. I believe that every skill — whether athletic, artistic, or culinary — is really just another way of paying attention to the world.

Most meaningfully, I've used music to connect with people. Through the Memories to Music program, I helped turn a stranger's life story into an original song. That experience changed how I think about creativity: it's not about the product, it's about the person on the other side.

"The meaning of life is to find your gift. The purpose of life is to give it away."

Music & Songwriting

From school jazz band to composing for a cause — music is how I connect.

Memories to Music · 2026

Composing "Pursuit of a Journey"

As lead vocalist of a three-piece band, I participated in Mentor College's annual Memories to Music program — an 18-year tradition pairing high school musicians with individuals living with early-stage Alzheimer's. Over five weeks, my bandmates and I interviewed our senior partner, learned her life story, and composed an original song reflecting her journey. We performed it live at the year-end concert, and she wrote us a thank-you letter I'll keep forever.

William wrote this song after spending time with a senior partner in the Memories to Music program. Written from her perspective and performed by William, the song traces a life shaped by courage, reinvention, and love — from leaving everything behind to building a new home, from raising a family to finding her own voice on stage. The excerpts below capture the song's core message.

Writing these lines, I realized I wasn't just telling someone else's story — I was hearing echoes of my own. The line "working hard is my choice" became a mirror: training six hours a week as badminton captain, folding paper until the creases were right, practicing a sax riff until it felt natural — each is its own small pursuit of a journey.

And in the lines about music running in the family, about learning songs by ear and passing them on, I understood why this project exists. Music isn't just sound — it's how we hold onto memory, how we share who we are. Her story reminded me that growth isn't about talent alone. It's about showing up, trusting the process, and passing what you've learned to the people beside you.

On the court

Badminton

Team captain, mixed doubles competitor, and two-time MVP.

Leading by Example

As captain of the Mentor College badminton team, I've helped select players, organize practices, and compete in mixed doubles at the regional level — placing 5th out of 40 teams at the 2026 Region of Peel tournament.

What I love most isn't the winning — it's the split-second decisions, the trust between partners, and the way a fast-paced rally clears your mind. Training six hours a week taught me that consistency beats talent when talent doesn't show up.

From my kitchen

Cooking & Baking

The kitchen is my laboratory — and my way of feeding the people I care about.

Food That Brings People Together

Cooking has been one of my favorite ways to unwind since I was young. In my school's cooking club, I've baked cookies and cakes, made fresh pasta, and perfected home-style dishes like mapo tofu and braised pork ribs with potatoes — the kind of food that fills a table and brings people together.

The club also gave me a way to give back: we sold baked goods and donated all proceeds to the local food bank. At home, I cook breakfast for my family every weekend. It's a small ritual, but it's how I show I care — one plate at a time.

My Signature Dish

Mapo tofu — silky tofu in a spicy, numbing sauce with ground pork. The trick is in the timing: add the Sichuan peppercorn oil at the very end so the aroma stays bright.

Summer 2026 · Baltimore, MD

Summer at Johns Hopkins

Two weeks on a college campus, learning beyond the classroom — and growing into a more independent version of myself.

I spent two weeks at Johns Hopkins University in their pre-college program, living in a dorm, navigating a new city, and taking classes alongside students from across the country. It was my first time away from home for an extended period — and I loved every minute of it.

Beyond academics, what stayed with me was the sense of responsibility: managing my own schedule, finding my way around Baltimore, and making decisions on my own. I also earned my CPR & AED certification while I was there — a skill I hope I'll never need, but one that makes me feel prepared for whatever comes.
